## Second hand gear

Second-hand gear has a charm to me. It not only uses technology that can be cool to revive and use in other creative projects, like old cassette machines, it is also a staple of its time. It holds a story, comes from an era where the technological limits were fairly different than our limits now. To me, that opens up possibilities by setting a hard ceiling. I like to call that a graspable ceiling, where you know what the limits are, and work between those. It helps with my creativity, but it also makes sure it does not get overwhelming. I will dive more into the philosophy of old gear in creativity, but at least you know what you might run into on this page.

### Reason for sharing all this.

Old gear can turn up on various places. From second hand stores to second hand sales sites, to attic cleanup runs. Whichever the case, if the gear is usable or can be made usable, it might be a candidate for tinkering. And, after some scouting on how to get it up and running again, I found that it could be valuable for others to share those journeys in repair, even if they failed. Who knows who can continue on failed projects, or finds value in completed projects.
Wanna learn more on limits and how software or tweaks can work with it? Read more on this in this blog-post.
Sorting all these quirks out is part of the process for me. And along the way, if I get it running or not, I might find new ceilings on a device, or figure out cool ways to work with it in other ways! Or, it simply looks cool on a shelf, as a relic of its time.
In some cases, that journey needs to be shared. For both the fun, but also a guidance for others who want to try out the same device, but might be stuck here and there.
Sharing is caring, right?

## The graspable ceiling

Old gear, with its ceiling, simply has a different charm to it. It often holds one purpose, and tries its best at that. Even when modern phones could do many of the same things, often way better (images, sounds, video/audio recording) to me it feels like removing a part of the charm. In some cases, you simply want a clean image or quality recording. But in some creative processes, devices with limits add charm and offer new creative ways. The less it can do, the more it needs your input. It is not right or wrong, just different. That is where most of my gear comes from. Old, broken, not used for 20+ years, blocked off by the doors of time, but still a beating heart inside. It can be very cool to recover or revive it! 

Check my projects to get an idea of what I am getting at, ha!

## From gear to tools

Some old tools are still quite usable as is. They work out of the box, and present you with their capabilities instantly. But, some tools used to have software to make things easier, or tweak stuff that is difficult to tweak from the outside. The [Yamaha SU10 ](/blog/su10-backup-and-facts)and the [Roland VS880](/blog/vs880-control-room) can do all of the work without software, but with the right software, it becomes even more interesting. The SU10 could be edited, backed up, and tweaked, and the Roland can even be controlled by an external midi controller!

Want more reverb during recording a live show? Twiddle a slider on a controller to make it so.

## What this blog is, and what it isn't

Now, what the blog is and what it isn't.
As the stories on this blog are basically my journey in tinkering, they are by no means definitive guides, or maintenance/repair guides. All tinkering is done on my own gear: if something broke, it was on me. that being said, as I don't want to break my gear obviously, I tried to include as much detail and information to make sure the process works in the best way, errors and odd bumps in the road included, but the choice of trying the same hacks or repairs on your own gear is fully yours (the readers).
Everything on this site worked on _my_ devices, with my cables, firmware, settings and software. Your setup might be different, and so might your results. Nothing here is "this is how it should be done, period". It's "this is what worked for me, with these devices, these settings and these tools". Nothing is hidden: the tools are open source (MIT) and listed in each post. Back up first, take your time, and enjoy finding your own way with it.
Software updates are not scheduled either. They happen during personal use, not on a frequent basis.

Enjoy your stay on this blog, and I hope the tools and information shared is of some value to you!
Create awesome things and share it with the world!
